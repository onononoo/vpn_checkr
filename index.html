    how it works :3

    your ip address is looked up with ipify and checked against known vpn providers, proxies, tor exit nodes and datacenters with ipquery.io and blackbox. home and mobile internet comes from an isp, while vpns almost always run on servers in a datacenter, so a datacenter address counts as a vpn. your location is not shown, because ip location databases are often wrong about vpn servers :3

    webrtc lets websites find your address for video calls, through its own connection. if that connection skips the vpn, it shows your real address. dns turns website names into addresses; bash.ws records which dns servers your browser used, and if they are not on your vpn's network, whoever runs them can see the sites you visit. ipv6 is a second kind of internet address, and some vpns only cover the older ipv4, so ipv6 traffic slips around them :3

    watch keeps checking your connection in the background, so you find out if your vpn drops without its kill switch catching it. if your browser allows notifications, you get one when something changes. the list of changes only lives in this tab and is gone when you close it. browsers slow down timers in background tabs, so checks there can take up to a minute, but it checks again as soon as you come back to the tab :3

    green means everything is fine, red means something is wrong, and grey means there was nothing to test, or the lookup services did not answer well enough to tell. no detection is perfect: small or private vpns can slip through, and some work or school networks can look like a vpn :p
